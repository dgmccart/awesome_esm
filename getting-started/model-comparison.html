Choose Your Model

Detailed comparison of ESM models.

Overview of ESM Models

Welcome! This guide will help you choose the right ESM model for your research.

The ESM model family includes three categories:

Category Models Purpose
Foundation models ESMC, ESM-2 Understand protein sequences and generate embeddings
Generative models ESM3 Design entirely new proteins
Structure prediction ESMFold, ESMFold2, ESMFold2-Fast Predict 3D protein structures

Quick Decision Guide

I want to understand what my protein sequences mean

Use ESMC

ESMC is the newest foundation protein language model. It was trained on 2.8 billion protein sequences and produces high-quality representations that capture protein biology from amino acid composition through function.

Best for

  • Protein embeddings
  • Machine learning feature extraction
  • Functional analysis
  • Representation learning
  • New research projects

Example

You have 100,000 enzyme sequences and want to predict activity. Use ESMC embeddings as features for your prediction model.

I want to generate a new protein

Use ESM3

ESM3 is a multimodal generative model that creates entirely new proteins from sequence, structure, and functional prompts.

Best for

  • Novel enzyme design
  • Protein engineering
  • Binder design
  • De novo protein generation
  • Exploring new regions of protein sequence space

Why ESM3?

  • Joint reasoning over sequence, structure, and function
  • Prompt-based protein generation
  • Supports multiple simultaneous design constraints
  • Generates proteins far beyond natural sequence diversity

Example

Design a protein that binds a specific therapeutic target while adopting a desired structural fold.

I need to predict protein structure

Choose one of the three structure prediction models.

ESMFold

Fast single-sequence prediction

Best for

  • Large databases
  • Metagenomic studies
  • Rapid screening

Advantages

  • No MSA required
  • 6–60× faster than AlphaFold2
  • Competitive accuracy

ESMFold2

Highest accuracy

Best for

  • Therapeutic proteins
  • Protein complexes
  • Antibody design
  • Detailed structural analysis

Advantages

  • State-of-the-art accuracy
  • Excellent complex prediction
  • Strong antibody–antigen performance

ESMFold2-Fast

Best balance of speed and accuracy

Best for

  • High-throughput prediction
  • Design iteration
  • Large screening projects

Advantages

  • Fastest inference
  • Excellent single-sequence performance
  • Near ESMFold2 accuracy

Structure Prediction Decision Table

Your situation Recommended model
More than 100,000 proteins ESMFold
Therapeutic protein design ESMFold2
Rapid design iteration ESMFold2-Fast
Antibody–antigen prediction ESMFold2
Budget-conscious large-scale prediction ESMFold2-Fast

I already use ESM-2

Keep ESM-2 if

  • Your production workflow already works.
  • You need reproducibility with published work.
  • Refactoring isn’t worthwhile.

Upgrade to ESMC if

  • You’re starting a new project.
  • You’re working with metagenomic sequences.
  • You want improved representations.

Practical Tips

Use the newest models

  • Prefer ESMC over ESM-2 for new projects.
  • Prefer ESM3 for protein generation.

Balance speed and accuracy

Goal Model
Fastest predictions ESMFold
Highest accuracy ESMFold2
Best compromise ESMFold2-Fast

Iterate during design

  • Use ESMFold2-Fast during rapid design cycles.
  • Use ESMFold2 to validate the best candidates.

Write clear prompts for ESM3

  • Be specific.
  • Combine structural and functional constraints.
  • Iterate and refine prompts.

Validate experimentally

High-confidence predictions indicate confidence in the predicted structure—not biological activity.

Experimental validation is always recommended.

Confidence Scores

All structure prediction models report pLDDT.

Score Interpretation
>0.90 Very high confidence
0.70–0.90 High confidence
0.50–0.70 Moderate confidence
<0.50 Low confidence

A low confidence score does not necessarily mean the protein is uninteresting—it indicates greater uncertainty in the predicted structure.

Key Takeaway

The ESM ecosystem provides complementary tools:

Model Purpose
ESMC Understand protein sequences
ESM3 Design new proteins
ESMFold Fast structure prediction
ESMFold2 Highest-accuracy structure prediction
ESMFold2-Fast High-throughput structure prediction

Many research workflows combine multiple ESM models. For example, you might use ESMC to analyze sequences, ESM3 to design new proteins, and ESMFold2 to predict the structures of your designs.
