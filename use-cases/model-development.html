Model Development

Papers on developing and advancing ESM models.

Papers in this section describe the models themselves and methodological advances that enable better representations of protein biology.

Title Description Link Model Usage
Protein Electrostatic Properties are Fine-Tuned
Through Evolution The study aims to predict the ionization states (pKa values) of amino acids in proteins directly from sequence alone, using protein language models (pLLMs) instead of requiring 3D structural data. Link ESM-C, ESM2, ESM3 Extracted per-residue embeddings from specific transformer layers of ESM2/ESMC models and fed them into a multilayer perceptron (MLP) to predict residue-specific pKa values — resulting in KaML-ESM. Sequence → ESM embeddings → MLP → pKa prediction
Training on test proteins improves fitness, structure, and function prediction This paper proposes a test-time training (TTT) framework for protein machine learning, where a pretrained protein model (e.g., ESM-family) is adapted on the single test protein sequence itself by minimizing its mask-prediction perplexity before downstream inference. This adaptation improves performance on tasks where distribution shift and data scarcity are limiting factors, including protein fitness prediction, structure prediction, and function classification. Link ESM-fold, ESM2, ESM3 They take pretrained ESM language models and fine-tune them at test time on the single query protein sequence, using the same self-supervised masked language modeling objective used in pretraining, effectively adapting the model to better represent that specific protein. The adapted model is then used unchanged for downstream predictions — for instance, improving structure prediction via ESMFold+TTT or fitness prediction via ESM-2+TTT, where test-time optimization reduces sequence perplexity and yields better task performance.
Learning millisecond protein dynamics from what is missing in NMR spectra This paper introduces Dyna-1, a deep learning model trained to predict millisecond-timescale (μs–ms) protein dynamics from missing backbone amide assignments in NMR spectra. The authors curated two major datasets — RelaxDB (133 proteins with experimental NMR relaxation data) and mBMRB (∼9,000 proteins with missing chemical shift assignments) — to train and evaluate their models. The key hypothesis is that missing peaks in NMR data often indicate residues undergoing exchange broadening due to conformational dynamics. Link ESM3 The best-performing model, Dyna-1, uses intermediate layer (layer 22) embeddings from ESM-3. These embeddings were critical in enabling the model to learn correlations between sequence/structure features and dynamic behavior. The model was benchmarked against ESM-2, AlphaFold, and other variants, and ESM-3 significantly outperformed others.
AI.zymes – A modular platform for evolutionary enzyme design AI.zymes is an integrated computational platform for de novo enzyme design. It combines multiple state-of-the-art tools (e.g. Rosetta, ESMFold, ProteinMPNN, etc.) in an iterative evolutionary algorithm to optimize enzyme sequences for improved catalytic properties In a case study, the system engineered variants of a ketosteroid isomerase enzyme, achieving a 7.7-fold increase in a target activity after only one design round. Link ESM-fold The platform incorporates ESMFold to predict protein structures during each design cyclehttps://www.researchgate.net/publication/388302117_AIzymes_-_A_modular_platform_for_evolutionary_enzyme_design#:~:text=engineering%20algorithms%20within%20an%20evolutionary,shot. This allows AI.zymes to evaluate and optimize sequence changes with structural context, drawing on ESM’s learned protein representations for accurate folding predictions.
TIGR-Tas: A family of modular RNA-guided DNA-targeting systems in prokaryotes and their viruses This paper identifies and characterizes a new family of RNA-guided DNA-targeting systems, called TIGR-Tas, encoded mostly in bacteriophages, archaeal viruses, and parasitic bacteria. These systems utilize a unique tandem-spacer architecture embedded in Tandem Interspaced Guide RNA (TIGR) arrays that guide associated proteins (Tas) to target DNA sequences. The system features novel combinations of RNA and protein domains, some with nucleases (HNH, RuvC), and demonstrates programmable genome editing, including in human cells. Link ESM-fold, ESM2 ESM-2 (15B) was used to generate protein embeddings from the final transformer layer, which informed community detection and clustering of novel Nop domain proteins—crucial to discovering TIGR-Tas systems.
Integrating Protein Language Models and Automatic Biofoundry for Enhanced Protein Evolution This study describes a biofoundry-integrated pipeline where AI-generated proteins are automatically synthesized, expressed, and tested using robotics. The pipeline was evaluated by evolving enzymes for enhanced catalytic activity using a minimal experimental dataset. The system allows rapid iteration between model generation and experimental testing. Link ESM-1v, ESM2 ESM-1v and ESM-2 were used to generate protein sequences with improved function, guided by embeddings and structure-based metrics. These models helped prioritize sequences for lab testing.
Enhancing the Efficiency of Protein Language Models with Minimal Wet-Lab Data through Few-Shot Learning The authors explored how few-shot learning can adapt large protein language models using minimal wet-lab experimental data. They tested their method (FSFP) across various tasks like fluorescence prediction and solubility, showing generalization from tiny data samples. Their workflow links lab data to rapid reweighting of model representations. Link ESM-1v, ESM2 They fine-tuned ESM-1v and ESM-2 on a limited number of experimentally annotated sequences. This reduced the need for large-scale retraining or lab screening.
Predicting Metal-binding Proteins and Structures Through Integration of Evolutionary-scale and Physics-based Modeling The authors present ESMBind, a two-stage workflow that combines ESM-2 and ESM-IF with physics-based modeling to predict metal-binding residues and generate 3D protein–metal complex structures. The method begins with deep learning–based residue-level predictions, then uses a local grid search and energy minimization to place metal ions realistically within protein structures. ESMBind was tested across seven biologically important metals and applied to predict metal-binding functions in uncharacterized fungal pathogen proteins. Link ESM-IF, ESM2 ESMBind integrates two ESM models:

• ESM-2 (650M): used for sequence embeddings, capturing evolutionary-scale sequence information. • ESM-IF: used for structure-aware embeddings, encoding geometric and structural features. These embeddings are fused and fed into a deep learning model that predicts per-residue metal-binding probabilities. This use of ESM models enables accurate, scalable, and generalizable predictions without requiring protein-specific retraining | | DyAb: sequence-based antibody design and property prediction in a low-data regime | DyAb is a deep learning framework for antibody engineering that excels with very limited training data. Built atop a pre-trained protein language model, it predicts changes in antibody binding affinity (and other properties) by modeling pairs of antibody sequences rather than absolute values. In tests on three antibody–antigen systems, DyAb achieved high correlation (Spearman ρ up to 0.85) between predicted and measured affinity changes using as few as ~100 samples. It also generated new antibody sequences via an embedded genetic algorithm, some of which showed >10-fold higher binding affinity than the original leads | Link | ESM2 | DyAb is built on a pre-trained protein language model (one option evaluated was Meta’s ESM-2), which provides rich sequence embeddings. These ESM-derived representations capture evolutionary and biophysical features of antibodies, which DyAb uses to predict affinity changes upon mutation. In essence, ESM’s learned protein context enables DyAb to identify subtle sequence–function relationships and propose beneficial mutations even with limited experimental data. | | Using artificial intelligence to document the hidden RNA virosphere | The study presents LucaProt, a transformer-based deep learning model that integrates protein sequence and structural information to identify highly divergent RNA-dependent RNA polymerases (RdRPs), which are signature markers of RNA viruses. Using LucaProt on over 10,000 metatranscriptomes from global ecosystems, the team uncovered 161,979 potential RNA virus species and 180 viral supergroups, significantly expanding the known RNA virosphere. | Link | ESM2 | The model incorporates ESM2-3B to extract residue-level structural embeddings of protein sequences. These embeddings are key to the model’s success in identifying highly divergent RdRP domains that traditional tools miss. | | Target sequence-conditioned design of peptide binders using masked language modeling | Presents PepMLM, a deep learning framework that designs de novo peptide binders to arbitrary protein targets solely from sequence, without requiring structural information. It achieves this by finetuning the ESM-2 protein language model to reconstruct masked binder sequences conditioned on target protein sequences, generating peptides that exhibit low perplexity and strong predicted and experimental binding to disease-relevant targets. | Link | ESM2 | PepMLM finetunes ESM-2 with a span masking strategy that places the entire peptide binder region as masked tokens appended to target protein sequences; the model is trained to reconstruct the masked binder conditional on the target. During generation, the target sequence plus mask tokens are fed into the model, which then decodes peptide sequences predicted to bind the target. | | Disease diagnostics using machine learning of B cell and T cell receptor sequences | This study develops MAchine Learning for Immunological Diagnosis (Mal-ID), a framework that uses immune receptor sequencing (BCR and TCR) combined with machine learning and protein language model embeddings to classify disease states from human blood samples. They show that patterns in immune receptor repertoires can accurately distinguish among infections (e.g., SARS-CoV-2, influenza), autoimmune diseases (e.g., lupus, type 1 diabetes), and vaccination status with high performance. | Link | ESM2 | The authors computed ESM-2 latent representations for each BCR and TCR sequence and then trained specialized machine learning classifiers (separate for IGHV/isotype groups in BCRs and for TRBV groups in TCRs) to predict disease states from those embeddings. These learned representations captured meaningful structural and immunological features that improved disease classification performance when integrated into the Mal-ID framework. | | De novo design of peptide binders to conformationally diverse targets with contrastive language modeling | This paper presents PepPrCLIP, a purely sequence-based pipeline for de novo design of short peptide binders to protein targets using a combination of protein language model–based generation and CLIP-style contrastive ranking sciadv.adr8638. The authors experimentally validate that PepPrCLIP-designed peptides can bind, inhibit, and induce degradation of structured enzymes (UltraID), transcriptional regulators (β-catenin), and highly disordered fusion oncoproteins (SS18-SSX1) in vitro | Link | ESM2 | They use ESM-2-650M embeddings in two places: (1) to generate naturalistic peptide candidates by applying Gaussian noise to residue-level ESM-2 latent embeddings and decoding them back into amino acid sequences sciadv.adr8638; (2) as frozen input features to a CLIP-style contrastive model, where peptide and target protein sequences are embedded with ESM-2, averaged across sequence length, passed through small MLP encoders, and optimized to maximize cosine similarity for true binding pairs sciadv.adr8638. Importantly, ESM-2 is not fine-tuned; it is used as a frozen pretrained embedding model. | | Engineering of CRISPR-Cas PAM recognition using deep learning of vast evolutionary data | This paper introduces Protein2PAM, a protein language model-based framework for predicting PAM (protospacer-adjacent motif) specificities of CRISPR-Cas systems directly from protein sequences. The model was trained on a massive dataset of over 45,000 PAMs across CRISPR-Cas Types I, II, and V, enabling in silico deep mutational scanning and prediction of PAM-interacting residues without structural information. | Link | ESM2 | The authors used a pretrained ESM-2 protein language model (650M parameters) as the backbone of Protein2PAM. This transformer model encoded the Cas protein sequences and was fine-tuned during training to predict nucleotide distributions at PAM positions. | | Protein codes promote selective subcellular compartmentalization | The authors developed ProtGPS, a model that identifies sequence motifs (“protein codes”) responsible for directing proteins into specific biomolecular condensates. They revealed a hidden code that determines localization to structures like the nucleolus. | Link | ESM2 | ProtGPS leveraged ESM-2 to learn sequence grammar in disordered regions, which was key to identifying these subtle localization codes. The ESM-derived representations enabled pattern discovery that traditional models had missed. | | Learning the language of antibody hypervariability | This paper presents AbMAP, a transfer learning framework that fine-tunes protein language models to specialize in antibody variable regions, especially CDRs. It uses structural and binding data to adapt general models to the antibody domain. | Link | ESM2 | The foundation model used was ESM-2, which was fine-tuned to better represent the highly variable regions of antibodies. ESM’s embeddings helped AbMAP learn nuanced sequence–structure–function relationships in immune proteins. | | Rapid in silico directed evolution by a protein language model with EVOLVEpro | The authors introduce EVOLVEpro, a computational framework that uses a protein language model to guide directed evolution of proteins. It couples ESM-based embeddings with a regression model to iteratively suggest beneficial mutations. | Link | ESM2 | ESM-2 was used to generate sequence embeddings rich in evolutionary context, enabling the model to predict functional mutations across varied protein families. Its output served as the foundation for active learning cycles in EVOLVEpro. | | DiffDock: Diffusion Steps, Twists, and Turns for Molecular Docking | uses ESM2 embeddings as input nodes features to a graph-neural net that generates ligand-binding pose predictions: DiffDock introduces a diffusion generative model for molecular docking that directly samples ligand poses in the protein binding site using the physical degrees of freedom (position, orientation, torsion). It outperforms previous methods in accuracy and speed and demonstrates strong generalization to folded protein structures like those generated by ESMFold. | Link | ESM2 | ESM (specifically ESMFold) was used to generate approximate protein structures (apo-structures) for testing. These embeddings were input to the score and confidence models to improve docking predictions. | | Designing Diverse and High-Performance Proteins with a Large Language Model in the Loop | The authors proposed a new closed-loop framework for protein design that combines LLMs with in-the-loop screening to iteratively improve protein candidates. They generated thousands of protein sequences and selected a subset for wet-lab synthesis and testing. Their focus was on achieving high diversity and performance across multiple protein functions. | Link | ESM2 | They used ESM2 to generate and score diverse protein candidates, integrating the model as a backbone for de novo design. ESM predictions were filtered using additional functional constraints before moving to synthesis. | | A General Temperature-Guided Language Model to Design Proteins of Enhanced Stability and Activity | This study presents a novel approach that integrates temperature constraints into the fine-tuning of ESM-2, creating a language model capable of designing proteins with tailored thermal stability. The authors conducted three rounds of mutation, model refinement, and synthesis to create enzyme variants. These were tested in the lab for thermostability and retained biological activity under stress. | Link | ESM2 | ESM-2 was fine-tuned to incorporate temperature sensitivity for stability design. | | FusPB-ESM2: Fusion Model of ProtBERT and ESM-2 for Cell-Penetrating Peptide Prediction | The authors developed a hybrid deep learning framework that combines ESM-2 and ProtBERT for predicting cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs). The model was trained on curated peptide datasets and tested both computationally and experimentally. Top predictions were synthesized and validated via uptake assays in mammalian cells. | Link | ESM2 | ESM-2 was used to extract high-dimensional representations (embeddings) of peptide sequences. These embeddings were fused with ProtBERT’s contextual outputs for robust classification. | | PTM-Mamba: a PTM-aware protein language model with bidirectional gated Mamba blocks | introduces PTM-Mamba, a protein language model that explicitly represents post-translational modifications (PTMs) by adding PTM tokens and modeling sequences with bidirectional Mamba (state-space) blocks fused with pretrained ESM-2 embeddings. The model is designed to encode both wild-type and PTM-annotated sequences and is evaluated on downstream tasks including disease association, druggability prediction, PTM effects on protein–protein interactions, and zero-shot PTM discovery | Link | ESM2 | They pass wild-type amino acid tokens through ESM-2-650M to obtain embeddings, while PTM tokens are mapped to for the ESM input, and the full PTM-tokenized sequence is processed by PTM-Mamba’s own embedding layer.
