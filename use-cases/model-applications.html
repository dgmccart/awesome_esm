Digital Los Alamos Game The author built a multi-agent simulation using ESM3 to explore autonomous scientific discovery. Ten AI agents with domain expertise collaborate across hubs like Health and Defense to design proteins for moonshot projects. Using a two-step ESM3 process—sequence generation and structure prediction—they iterate on designs with minimal human input. The system runs in Unity and uses GPT-4o for dialogue and O3-mini for reasoning. Link ESM3 They use ESM3 to generate full protein sequences from masked inputs based on Pfam motifs, then predict their structures to assess design quality using pTM confidence scores. If confidence is low, agents update their memory and iterate on the design in a closed-loop process.

In silico evolution of globular protein folds from random sequences Hi Everyone! I am a visiting postdoc at NIH/NCBI in the Evolutionary Genomics Research Group (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/groups/koonin/). My background is molecular modeling and biophysics, and I am trying to develop a simulation method that imitates protein fold evolution. The goal is to study how protein folds originate de novo from random sequences and how they evolve. The basic idea is that we introduce mutations in a population of random amino acid sequences (at this stage, usually short sequences 50-70 aa), evaluate the effect of these mutations on protein structure with ESMfold, select a new set of proteins for further evolution, and repeat the cycle. More details in this https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.10.622830v1.A peculiar phenomenon is that under selective pressure, random sequences that mutate stochastically evolve into novel folds, often with very high pLDDT and pTM scores. Other interesting things include the extraction of evolutionary information from simulations that can be provided to AlphaFold or the estimation of protein origination time.I hope to talk with you about how reliable the pLDDT and pTM scores can be in this situation and what other approaches can be used to evaluate the predicted structures. For example, we performed MD simulations, which show that not all proteins with high pLDDT are stable, but this method is slow and expensive.Once the work is published, we will want to switch to the ESM-C, and I would appreciate your recommendations and comments. A 39.8kb flavi-like virus uses a novel strategy for overcoming the RNA virus error threshold This study reports the discovery of a 39.8kb RNA virus—the longest known flavi-like virus—identified from a Haliclona sponge metatranscriptome. Unlike other large RNA viruses (e.g., Nidovirales), this virus lacks known proofreading exonuclease domains but appears to encode bacterial-like genes involved in nucleic acid metabolism, possibly supporting genome expansion. Link ESM-fold The authors used ESMFold to model portions of the viral polyprotein. These predictions, combined with AlphaFold, helped identify and annotate conserved and novel protein domains, including structural homologs to known viral proteins and unique bacteria-like components.

De novo design of peptide binders to conformationally diverse targets with contrastive language modeling Designing binders to target undruggable proteins presents a formidable challenge in drug discovery. In this work, we provide an algorithmic framework to design short, target-binding linear peptides, requiring only the amino acid sequence of the target protein. To do this, we propose a process to generate naturalistic peptide candidates through Gaussian perturbation of the peptidic latent space of the ESM-2 protein language model and subsequently screen these novel sequences for target-selective interaction activity via a contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP)–based contrastive learning architecture. By integrating these generative and discriminative steps, we create a Peptide Prioritization via CLIP (PepPrCLIP) pipeline and validate highly ranked, target-specific peptides experimentally, both as inhibitory peptides and as fusions to E3 ubiquitin ligase domains. PepPrCLIP-derived constructs demonstrate functionally potent binding and degradation of conformationally diverse, disease-driving targets in vitro. In total, PepPrCLIP empowers the modulation of previously inaccessible proteins without reliance on stable and ordered tertiary structures. Link ESM2 nan

The Virtual Lab: AI Agents Design New SARS-CoV-2 Nanobodies with Experimental Validation This paper introduces the “Virtual Lab,” a framework where a team of large language model (LLM) agents, guided by a human researcher, collaboratively conducts complex scientific research. The framework is demonstrated on a real-world task: designing new nanobodies that bind to recent SARS-CoV-2 variants using computational tools like ESM, AlphaFold-Multimer, and Rosetta. Link ESM-1b ESM-1b was used to compute log-likelihood ratios of point mutations in nanobody sequences, identifying mutations most likely to improve nanobody quality. The LLM agents scripted a full ESM-based mutation ranking pipeline that guided design iterations.
Genome-wide prediction of disease variant effects with a deep protein language model This study presents a scalable method to predict the effects of all possible missense mutations in the human genome using ESM1b, a 650M-parameter protein language model. The authors created a workflow that overcomes ESM1b’s length limitations, enabling variant effect prediction across ~42,000 human protein isoforms, covering ~450 million potential mutations. Link ESM-1b They used ESM1b to compute log-likelihood ratio (LLR) scores for each missense mutation, quantifying how likely a mutation is given evolutionary sequence patterns. This approach does not require multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) and is generalizable to all proteins, including those lacking homology.
Protein language model-guided engineering of an anti-CRISPR protein for precise genome editing in human cells Used protein language models to guide the engineering of the anti-CRISPR protein AcrIIA4, aiming to improve CRISPR/Cas9 precision. By combining these models with high-throughput screening, they tested ~10,000 variants and identified ones that blocked off-target edits while preserving on-target activity — offering a path to safer gene editing. Link ESM-1v In the paper, ESM-1v was used to identify functionally important regions (hotspots) in the anti-CRISPR protein AcrIIA4. By scoring how likely a given mutation is under the model, the authors pinpointed residues where changes would most likely affect protein function. These predicted hotspots were then targeted for focused mutagenesis, drastically narrowing the search space and guiding the design of a ~10,000-variant library for experimental screening — enabling efficient discovery of variants that improve CRISPR precision.
De novo designed proteins neutralize lethal snake venom toxins Researchers used deep learning-based protein design to create de novo proteins capable of neutralizing three-finger toxins (3FTxs) — the key lethal components in elapid snake venoms (e.g., cobras, kraits, mambas). These proteins were designed from scratch (i.e., they have no natural analogs), and were engineered to bind tightly to: • Short-chain α-neurotoxins • Long-chain α-neurotoxins • Cytotoxins They achieved this using computational screening and a small number of experimental iterations to identify the most effective designs Link ESM-IF ESM models, specifically the structure-generating ESM-IF1, were used to design de novo proteins that structurally complement and bind tightly to lethal snake venom toxins. The model enabled accurate inverse folding to generate protein sequences with predicted atomic-resolution structures optimized for high-affinity toxin binding.
Enhancing Antibody Language Models with Structural Information This study used ESM-IF1 to fine-tune antibody sequences. Structural context was integrated to better predict binding properties, and the top sequences were tested for antigen specificity and binding strength in vitro. Link ESM-IF nan
Predicting absolute protein folding stability using generative models Shows that ESM-IF can accurately predict absolute protein folding stability using the summed likelihood of the native sequence. Without any retraining, the model achieves competitive accuracy on a benchmark of experimentally measured protein stabilities, offering a promising tool for protein design and biophysics. Link ESM-IF The authors used ESM-IF (Inverse Folding model from the ESM family) to predict absolute protein folding stability (ΔG). Specifically, they summed the log-likelihoods assigned by ESM-IF to the wild-type amino acid sequence given a protein structure and found this sum correlates with experimental ΔG values. The approach assumes that likelihood under ESM-IF reflects fitness and stability, and they benchmarked it against other models like ESM-1b, FoldX, and Chroma.

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis Looking to see if text and protein models “converge” on the same representation We argue that representations in AI models, particularly deep networks, are converging. First, we survey many examples of convergence in the literature: over time and across multiple domains, the ways by which different neural networks represent data are becoming more aligned. Next, we demonstrate convergence across data modalities: as vision models and language models get larger, they measure distance between datapoints in a more and more alike way. Link nan nan
